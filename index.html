§01

Overview

Why decentralized social media changes the benchmark

Bluesky's AT Protocol separates identity, hosting, relays, and moderation across independent actors. That makes public data collection more reproducible — but it also means model performance cannot be judged apart from how labels were produced, validated, and governed.

Decentralized social platforms create new opportunities and challenges for computational mental health research because data access, moderation, labeling, and deployment responsibilities are distributed across multiple technical and governance layers. This study treats mental health disclosure detection as a system-level benchmarking problem — not a leaderboard exercise.

End-to-end pipeline

Bluesky firehose collection, weak labeling, human validation, and benchmarking in one auditable system.

Decentralized setting

Evaluates access, reproducibility, deployment, and governance implications specific to AT Protocol data.

Dual-task benchmarking

SI and MH models compared on performance, validation sensitivity, and computational efficiency.

Label audit

Human validation reveals task-specific weak-label errors: false negatives for SI, false positives for MH.

§03

Corpus Construction

Two task-specific Bluesky corpora

Collected May–June 2025 via lexicon-triggered firehose retrieval. Task construction — not just modeling — shapes downstream interpretation.

Suicidal Ideation (SI) corpus

14-DAY WINDOW · 6,648 UNIQUE USERS · 1.3 POSTS/USER
SI-positive2,180 posts · 26.1%
SI-negative6,166 posts · 73.9%

Narrower construct: first-person desire to die, passive death wish, self-harm ideation, or planning language. Lower positive rate despite a suicide-focused lexicon — many matching posts discuss public events, fiction, jokes, news, or other people.

Mental Health (MH) corpus

2-MONTH WINDOW · 9,988 LABELED POSTS
MH-positive4,793 posts · 48.0%
MH-negative5,195 posts · 52.0%

Broader construct: depression, anxiety, panic, insomnia, hopelessness, and help-seeking. Higher, more balanced positive rate — part of why MH models score well against weak labels even though human agreement is weaker.

Note — only publicly available Bluesky posts were used. The benchmark is intended for aggregate research and model evaluation — not clinical diagnosis or automated individual-level intervention. The unit of analysis is the post, not the user; the pipeline uses no longitudinal history, reply context, or profile metadata.

Annotation boundary rules

SI
In scope

First-person desire to die, passive death wish, current self-harm ideation, planning language, or recent self-directed suicidal intent.

Excluded

News about suicide, advocacy posts, condolences, quotations, jokes, hypotheticals, third-person discussion.

MH
In scope

First-person disclosure of depression, anxiety, panic, hopelessness, distress-related insomnia, help-seeking, or self-harm-related distress.

Excluded

Awareness posts, clinical information sharing, political commentary, metaphors, sarcasm, statements about another person.

Ambiguous content
Positive only if

Self-directed disclosure was explicit enough to support the task definition.

Negative if

Context was insufficient, quoted, purely rhetorical, or not attributable to the author.

§06

Cross-Task Synthesis

SI and MH are not interchangeable detection tasks

SI targets a narrow, self-disclosure construct with stronger human agreement but is vulnerable to subtle false negatives. MH captures a broader weak-labeling function that is easier for models to reproduce than for humans to accept as strict self-disclosure.

SI benchmark

Construct
Narrower — requires self-directed suicidal ideation or passive desire to die.
Dominant risk
False negatives for subtle, indirect, or context-dependent SI.
Best signal
BERT+LSTM under stratified CV; RoBERTa under holdout.
Efficiency
DistilBERT — strong holdout performance at lowest transformer training time.
Deployment
Requires conservative interpretation and high-sensitivity human review if used for screening research.

MH benchmark

Construct
Broader — includes self-directed mental health symptoms and distress.
Dominant risk
False positives for awareness, advocacy, metaphor, humor, third-person discussion.
Best signal
DistilRoBERTa under stratified 5-fold CV.
Efficiency
TinyBERT is fastest; MiniLM offers the strongest speed–performance balance.
Deployment
Requires calibration to avoid over-classifying general discourse as self-disclosure.

Structured error sources

SI false negatives

Short posts, indirect death-wish language, quoted or context-dependent statements, ambiguous expressions of despair that are pragmatic rather than lexical signals.

MH false positives

Awareness campaigns, discussion of public figures, support messages directed at others, and metaphorical uses of terms like "depressing" or "panic."

§07

Deployment & Governance

Who can run this, and what should it be used for?

In AT Protocol-style architectures, hosting, relays, clients, labelers, and algorithms may all be run by different actors — a mental health classifier could serve very different roles depending on who deploys it.

Defensible near-term use

Aggregate research and tool evaluation. Compact models (DistilBERT, MiniLM, TinyBERT) lower the barrier for independent researchers and community operators to run and audit the system.

Requires safeguards

Any individual-level SI use needs human review, crisis-response protocols, opt-in governance, and clear accountability for false positives and false negatives.

Not appropriate

Automated MH intervention — many positive weak labels correspond to public discourse rather than genuine personal distress.

Note — efficiency should not be confused with readiness for intervention. Compact models can reproduce weak labels quickly, but sensitive deployment still requires human-grounded calibration, transparent oversight, and task-specific validation.
§08

Limitations & Future Work

What this benchmark does not (yet) show

Keyword-triggered retrieval+
Lexicon-based collection increases the density of relevant content but may miss implicit, coded, or context-dependent disclosures — particularly indirect SI expressed across reply chains. Future work: semantic retrieval, classifier-in-the-loop sampling, active learning.
Limited human-grounded validation+
Validation covered only stratified subsets. The low chance-corrected MH agreement (κ = 0.222) means MH scores should not be read as estimates of performance against a human-grounded construct. Future work needs larger human-grounded test sets and diverse annotators.
Post-level, not longitudinal+
The benchmark does not model user trajectories, repeated disclosures, escalation, or temporal language change. Longitudinal modeling could help risk-sensitive analysis but raises stronger privacy and consent concerns.
No interpretability or ablations+
The evaluation emphasizes predictive performance and efficiency but lacks token attribution, counterfactual editing, and component-level ablations of retrieval, prompts, and validation sampling.
Time- and platform-bound findings+
Results reflect Bluesky during the May–June 2025 collection window and may shift with platform language, demographics, affordances, and moderation practices. Replication across windows and other AT Protocol services is needed.
